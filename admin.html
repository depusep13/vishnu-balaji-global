Components & Operating Principles of a Trade CRM

An export-oriented Customer Relationship Management (CRM) system manages international buyers, trade compliance milestones, logistics workflows, and pricing. Below is an overview of how this system implements the core operational areas:

1. Sales & Deal Pipeline

Deals are tracked through specific shipment phases (RFQ -> FOB Quoting -> L/C Verification -> SGS Inspection -> Loading -> Port Export). This ensures no container transaction is lost.

2. Marketing & Leads Acquisition

Utilizes customized email quotation templates and AI-powered buyer discovery tools (matching customs declarations) to proactively generate new sales funnels.

3. Service & Trade Inquiry Logging

Every lead submitted from the frontend is registered in a secure database. If the database is offline, it routes details to WhatsApp, keeping communication pathways active.

4. Commerce & Quotation Calculations

A shipping calculator helps model raw domestic costs in INR, port charges, ocean freight, and target margins into a unified CIF price sheet for foreign buyers.

5. Data Management & Governance

Combines cloud storage (Supabase Rest API) with localStorage caching to guarantee access to logs even during local network dropouts, with a one-click spreadsheet download.

6. Automation & Auto-Replies

A keyword-matching trade chatbot handles initial client specs inquiries in real-time, pre-qualifying leads before handing them over to direct sales representatives.

7. Analytics & Reporting

Displays KPI metrics tracking total volume (Tonnage), country density, and item ratios, converting quantities into metric tons for volume analysis.

Structured Guide

Follow this sequential documentation roadmap. Click on any phase to view details. You can view the full SOP file in the repository at export_sop.md.

Phase 1: Pre-Shipment & Sales Contract
Documents: Proforma Invoice (PI), Purchase Order (PO), Sales Contract.
Action: Register and verify buyer specifications, price in Cost Analyzer, and get signed bilateral contracts. Lock HS Codes.
Phase 2: Trade Finance & Bank Setup
Documents: Letter of Credit (L/C) Draft, Bank AD Code registration letter.
Action: Check L/C draft for typographical spelling errors in consignee name. File and register bank AD Code on ICEGATE.
Phase 3: Pre-Shipment Inspection & Quality
Documents: SGS/Intertek Certificate, Phytosanitary Certificate, COA.
Action: Arrange third-party sampling before container sealing. File for Phytosanitary clearings (required for agro products) and fumigation.
Phase 4: Customs Entry & LEO Approval
Documents: Commercial Invoice, Packing List, Shipping Bill, Let Export Order (LEO).
Action: File Shipping Bill via Customs Broker (CHA) on ICEGATE under LUT. Obtain customs clearance LEO to authorize vessel loading.
Phase 5: Transport Carriage & Transit
Documents: Bill of Lading (B/L) or AWB, Marine Insurance Certificate.
Action: Carrier issues B/L upon vessel departure. Ensure B/L matches L/C requirements exactly before forwarding to partner bank.
Phase 6: Incentives & Payment Realization
Documents: Certificate of Origin (COO), e-BRC / e-FIRC, RoDTEP Claim.
Action: Obtain Chamber of Commerce COO. Present shipping docs to bank. Once funds clear, retrieve e-BRC to claim duty drawback / RoDTEP.

Fraud Prevention — Red Flags

Critical
Buyer pays 100% after delivery
NEVER ship without confirmed advance payment or irrevocable L/C. This is the #1 fraud trap for new exporters.
Buyer sends fake L/C or SWIFT MT103
Always verify L/C authenticity via your AD Bank (not via buyer's bank alone). Check SWIFT BIC codes independently.
Buyer uses Gmail / Yahoo address
Insist on a corporate domain email. Verify their company on LinkedIn, trade portals, or Chamber of Commerce registrations.
No video call / refuses inspection
Any serious buyer will allow pre-shipment SGS inspection or video call verification. Refusal = red flag.
Pressure to ship quickly / skip docs
"Ship urgently, documents later" is a classic manipulation tactic. Never compromise on documentation.
✔ Verified: ECGC Credit Insurance
Cover your export receivables with ECGC (Export Credit Guarantee Corporation) to protect against buyer default.

TT Wire Transfer (Telegraphic Transfer) — Safe Practices

Module 8
Receiving TT Advance
  • Always provide YOUR OWN bank SWIFT code in PI
  • Confirm receipt via AD Bank before production
  • Check currency: USD preferred, not local currency
  • Bank deducts 1–2% as transfer charges — clarify who pays
  • Minimum 30% advance before loading container
e-BRC & FIRC After TT
  • e-FIRC = Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Download from your AD Bank after TT credit
  • Required to claim RoDTEP / Duty Drawback
  • File on DGFT portal within 12 months of shipment
  • Keep e-BRC for 5 years (audit requirement)
Approved Countries for TT
  • UAE, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait — TT very common
  • Malaysia, Indonesia — safe for TT with known buyers
  • UK, Germany — demand L/C for first orders
  • Africa (Nigeria, Kenya) — always demand 50%+ advance
  • Avoid: sanctioned countries (OFAC list check always)

Negotiation Tips

1. Anchor High: Quote 5–8% above your target price. Buyers always negotiate down.
2. Never reveal cost: Quote FOB or CIF, not your sourcing cost.
3. Minimum Order: Start with 1 container (25 MT) for new buyers.
4. SGS is your power: "SGS certified before loading" builds instant trust.
5. Create urgency: "Quote valid for 7 days — subject to port slot availability."
6. Payment first: Always demand payment confirmation before container booking.
